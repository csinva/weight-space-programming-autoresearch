I read all 24 `weights/G*.txt` files and made a prediction for every edit. These are rough estimates, not exact values: the files give the gains and the embedding columns, but not the model's normalized hidden value at each dimension for each prompt, and that value sets both the sign and the size of the effect.

**How I estimated:**
- **Size of the change:** each edit adds about 29·g·n_d·E[token,d] to each token's logit, where g is the gain and n_d is that missing hidden value. I assumed |n_d| ≈ 0.7. That makes the factor 85–125, depending on the gain, which ranges from 3.9 to 6.0.
- **Sign of n_d:** I picked it by checking which way common output tokens (newline, ",", " and", " the", ".") and the eight target tokens lean in each embedding column, relative to the column's mean. When those two signals disagreed (G05, G10, G20), I went with the common tokens. Those three edits are the most likely to have the sign wrong.
- **dlogp:** for each prompt I re-weighted the listed top-50 probabilities by exp(k·E), allowed for the tokens outside the top 50, and took minus the log of the new normaliser (written p_t + rest in the notes).
- **up:** I mixed the column's most extreme tokens in the chosen direction with high-probability top-50 tokens that have large E, since those are sure to reach p ≥ 0.001 on some prompt.
