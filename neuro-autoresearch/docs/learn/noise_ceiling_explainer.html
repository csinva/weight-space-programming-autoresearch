Untrained champion vs trained AlexNet, on the variance that's actually predictable

One number, one comparison. The score is the fraction of explained variance a model captures on the high-signal voxels of NSD's early + ventral visual cortex. Higher is better; 1.0 = the noisy data's own ceiling.

The metric, in one line

For each high-signal voxel: R² / NC_r2 = of the variance that was even predictable given the noise, what fraction did the model capture. The reward is the mean of that across the voxels above — the Algonauts 2023 convention. Reliability cut: NC_r ≥ 0.2, n = 3 repeats.

Reading it

This untrained chroma/spectral champion reaches AlexNet's level only in V1 (0.542 vs 0.540). Everywhere else AlexNet is ahead, and the gap widens steadily up the hierarchy — modest in early/retinotopic cortex (V2, V3, early stream) and large in the category-selective end (ventral, faces, places, words), where ImageNet training buys representations a fixed low-level feature bank can't reproduce.
