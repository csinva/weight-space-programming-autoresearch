neuro-autoresearch · field notes

Three windows
on the living brain

We teach machines to predict recorded brain activity. A stimulus is pushed through a fixed, never-trained model; a ridge regression reads those activations out against real neural recordings; the held-out correlation — test_corr — is the score. The same loop runs across wildly different ways of listening to the brain. These three guides explain the neuroscience behind each.

Each modality trades spatial reach against temporal speed differently — fMRI sees the whole brain but slowly, MEG is fast but blurry, ECoG and macaque spiking buy both at once by going inside. Reading the same encoding-model pipeline across all of them is what makes the contrasts legible.
